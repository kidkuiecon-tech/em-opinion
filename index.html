Global Macro Thesis

The global backdrop is not a simple risk-on or recession story. The house views cluster around a resilient macro setup: growth is still supported by US demand, capex and AI-linked activity; inflation pressure is easing enough to reduce imminent Fed-hike fear; but long-end yields, USD/JPY intervention, and crowded EM carry mean investors still need selectivity before moving into trade ideas.

01
Global growth: resilient enough, but not a clean Goldilocks regime
Why the macro setup still supports risk assets but does not remove constraints

The starting point for the dashboard should be that the global economy has not broken. Morgan Stanley’s base case still shows global GDP growth of 3.2% in 2026 and 3.3% in 2027, with EM growth higher at 4.5% and 4.4%. This matters because trade ideas should not be introduced as defensive recession trades; the macro base is still sufficiently resilient for carry, equities and duration to coexist.

However, the resilience is increasingly conditional. Morgan Stanley’s narrative relies on capex, manufacturing and AI-related demand, while HSBC argues that US activity and inflation surprises are now rolling over. That combination is supportive for front-end rates and risk assets, but it is not the same as synchronized global easing. The difference is important: the audience should understand that the opportunity set is tactical and selective, not a broad relaxation of macro constraints.

UBS adds a caution that is especially relevant for EM: US momentum looks stronger than much of EM, and EM leadership is narrow. That means investors can still like selective EM carry or local rates without saying that EM growth differentials have broadly re-accelerated. The macro environment is therefore best described as resilient global demand with narrow leadership and persistent rates sensitivity.

Views

Morgan Stanley Morgan Stanley keeps a constructive global outlook. Its latest key forecasts say the Fed should stay on hold for the remainder of 2026 and cut by 50bp in 2027, while the ECB is expected to hike in September and the BoJ normalization is pulled forward to October. Its global growth framing remains capex- and manufacturing-led, with Asia at the center and AI demand still supporting exports and investment.

HSBC HSBC describes the market tension as “Goldilocks or long-end pain?” rather than a pure risk-off setup. Its view is that long-end rates have become the main stress point, but softer US activity and inflation data should support the front end and allow risk assets, tech/growth and EMD to keep support if the curve bull-steepens rather than bear-steepens.

UBS UBS is less relaxed about EM breadth. It argues that global growth resilience is not translating evenly into EM FX because US momentum is improving relative to large parts of EM, while EM outperformance remains concentrated around Korea and Taiwan tech leadership.

02
Inflation and Fed path: the peak hawkish impulse is easing, but not gone
The front end can be supported even while policy uncertainty remains

The most important bridge from global macro to EM trades is the Fed path. The new source set does not support saying the Fed has turned decisively dovish. It supports a more nuanced statement: immediate hike fear has moderated, but markets remain data dependent and the USD still tracks near-term Fed pricing.

HSBC’s “front-end support” view is useful for explaining why EMD and tech/growth can still work even if long-end rates are volatile. Softer US data reduce the threat of imminent hikes, and that helps the short end. But Morgan Stanley’s and J.P. Morgan’s framing keeps the dashboard from over-interpreting this as a broad easing cycle: the ECB, BoJ and USD/JPY channels still matter.

For the trade section, the implication is that investors can take selective carry or receiver positions where they are compensated, but should keep stops and scenario discipline. The macro setup is less hostile than in June/July, but it is still sensitive to CPI, payrolls, Fed communication and long-end rate repricing.

Views

HSBC HSBC argues that US inflation and activity data are starting to underwhelm versus expectations. It does not claim inflation will drop suddenly to 2%, but it says the breadth of headline, core and supercore CPI components suggests the peak hawkish impulse may be behind markets.

Morgan Stanley Morgan Stanley also expects the Fed to stay on hold through 2026, but keeps central-bank divergence in the narrative: ECB hike risk in September and earlier BoJ normalization mean the global policy backdrop is not uniformly dovish.

J.P. Morgan J.P. Morgan’s FX outlook stresses that the dollar remains closely tied to near-term Fed expectations. It neutralized long-USD exposure after payrolls, but still argues that data dependence remains paramount and carry is the central component of the trading strategy.

03
Long-end rates: the key global risk that can still hurt EM duration and FX
A bull steepener is supportive; a bear steepener is the main pain point

The long end is the part of the global setup that the audience needs to understand before seeing duration trades. If the curve steepens because the front end rallies on weaker data while the long end is stable, that can support EMD. If the curve steepens because the long end sells off on fiscal/inflation risk, EM duration and high-beta FX are more vulnerable.

Morgan Stanley and HSBC are not identical here. Morgan Stanley’s cross-asset monitor emphasizes the repricing in long yields and the risk that the curve steepens further. HSBC’s multi-asset bulletin accepts the long-end pain but argues that front-end rates are well supported because US data should underwhelm. The dashboard should therefore frame the trade environment around the shape of the rates move, not only the level of yields.

This is also why Standard Chartered favors selective EM LCY exposure. When EM-DM yield spreads are already narrow, a lower oil/stable USD backdrop helps tactical carry, but it does not create much room for broad compression. Duration trades need country-level justification, curve logic and carry cushion.

Views

Morgan Stanley Morgan Stanley highlights that US 30-year yields crossed 5.2% for the first time since 2007 and warns that the US curve could steepen further unless inflation surprises higher and Fed Chair Warsh clarifies inflation remarks.

HSBC HSBC sees long-end pain as the risk behind a reverse-Goldilocks episode, but argues that if US activity continues to disappoint, front-end support should allow the UST curve to bull-steepen rather than trigger a generalized risk-asset drawdown.

Standard Chartered Standard Chartered’s EM rates framework also makes global yields central: rich EM valuations and elevated global yields mean carry and roll should drive returns more than broad yield compression.

04
Risk appetite and positioning: supportive, but narrow and flow-sensitive
The setup supports selective risk, not broad EM beta

Risk appetite is supportive enough to keep the discussion focused on opportunities, but the evidence does not justify a broad-beta conclusion. Morgan Stanley’s base allocation is risk-positive, HSBC’s short-term signals are not overbought, and corporate earnings sentiment is improving. But UBS provides the caveat: EM performance remains narrow and highly dependent on North Asia tech.

This matters for dashboard sequencing. The Global Macro tab should first show that risk assets are not breaking, then immediately explain why the opportunity set is selective. A simple “risk-on” label would hide the tension between strong US earnings/capex momentum and the less convincing breadth in EM growth and FX.

The investment implication is to separate three channels: global risk support, EM flow/technical support, and country-level macro quality. Only where these three overlap should the dashboard allow a stronger trade framing.

Views

Morgan Stanley Morgan Stanley’s cross-asset allocation remains broadly constructive: overweight global stocks with a US preference, equal-weight commodities, overweight govies overall after adding EGBs, underweight US IG and adding EUR IG on technicals and duration support.

HSBC HSBC’s sentiment/positioning framework is not sending a sell signal. Aggregate short-term indicators are below 20% in neutral territory, systematic sentiment is at the 50th percentile, and its regime radars show a Goldilocks backdrop building.

UBS UBS warns that the supportive global risk backdrop has not broadened cleanly into EM. EM equities have outperformed in headline terms, but the breadth is weaker once Korea/Taiwan tech and top contributors are separated out.

05
USD, JPY and carry: global FX is no longer one-way dollar bullish
Carry survives, but Fed and intervention risk keep ranges tight

The FX message is more balanced than a month ago. J.P. Morgan no longer wants broad USD length, but it does not call for a weak-dollar trend either; data dependence and Fed pricing still anchor the range. That makes carry more important, but also means carry has to be assessed against crowded positioning and country vulnerability.

UBS gives the cleanest warning for EM FX. Carry dominated recent returns, but only a minority of liquid EM currencies delivered positive six-month total returns against the USD. UBS also says a dovish or unchanged Fed is needed for EM FX performance to broaden. In other words, EM FX is not just waiting for lower US rates; it also needs better domestic breadth and cleaner external balances.

For ASEAN, Maybank and BofA/KKP push the same broad conclusion from different angles. Maybank emphasizes that ASEAN is less favored relative to other EM carry opportunities, while BofA/KKP emphasizes reserves, FX deposits and capital-flight channels. The dashboard should therefore not portray ASEAN FX as a uniform beneficiary of lower oil or lower USD; it should be a country-by-country story.

Views

J.P. Morgan J.P. Morgan neutralized long USD after payrolls because recent data, détente around US-Iran risk, global growth signs and renewed long-end sensitivity reduced confidence in the trade. It still retains a mild dollar-bullish bias and a 164 year-end USD/JPY target after intervention, but wants risk concentrated in carry rather than broad USD length.

UBS UBS says EM FX carry is crowded and faces three under-discussed challenges: EM central banks’ changing reaction functions, narrowing EM growth differentials versus the US, and the risk that EU-China trade frictions deepen Chinese export penetration into the rest of EM.

Maybank FX Maybank’s ASEAN FX deck directly frames ASEAN as “The Less Favored EM,” arguing that carry and fundamentals favor other EM more than ASEAN, with country differentiation around MYR, PHP, THB, IDR and SGD.

EM Macro Thesis

ASEAN/EM backdrop is improving tactically, but the message is selective carry and differentiated macro risk rather than broad EM risk-on.

01
ASEAN macro: the fog is lifting, but the region is still energy- and FX-sensitive
Improving sentiment does not remove oil/current-account vulnerability

The EM Macro Backdrop should start with ASEAN because the new source set contains several ASEAN-specific reports. The key message is not that ASEAN is suddenly a broad outperformer. It is that the macro fog is lifting at the global-sentiment level while ASEAN countries remain highly differentiated by energy exposure, external balances, reserves buffer and policy space.

Maybank’s “Fog Is Lifting” narrative helps set a constructive tone: world sentiment improved and uncertainty eased. But the same report highlights the energy channel. Most ASEAN-6 members are net oil-and-gas importers, with Thailand and Singapore most exposed on the net-energy-trade measure, while Malaysia is the only net energy exporter in the chart. That means lower crude helps, but the rebound in refined products and gas keeps the story from becoming a clean tailwind.

BofA/KKP adds the FX-reserves angle. Indonesia and the Philippines are more vulnerable because current-account deficits and capital outflows force central banks to choose between intervention, rate hikes and reserves preservation. Thailand is more resilient in reserves-buffer terms, but that does not mean THB is immune; it means the policy reaction function is different and less resistant to currency weakness.

Views

Maybank Maybank frames ASEAN Macro 2H26 as “The Fog Is Lifting.” The deck emphasizes improving world sentiment and easing uncertainty, but it also stresses that crude is lower than the March peak while natural gas and refined products are rebounding as ceasefire risk breaks down.

BofA / KKP BofA/KKP argues that ASEAN central banks are defending currencies with depleting FX reserve ammunition. The challenge is most acute for current-account deficit economies such as Indonesia and the Philippines, while Thailand remains the most resilient in reserves-buffer terms.

J.P. Morgan J.P. Morgan’s ASEAN update shows the region is not moving uniformly: Indonesia’s GDP beat and forecast upgrade are offset by a wider current-account deficit and BI hike risk; Thailand’s CPI undershoot supports BOT hold but negative real rates remain; the Philippines has a GDP miss and weaker growth forecast.

02
ASEAN policy room: easing is not synchronized; hold/hike risk still matters
Policy space differs sharply by country and by FX pressure

The audience should not go into trade ideas with the assumption that ASEAN central banks have broad room to cut. The new source set points to a patchwork: some inflation prints are improving, but FX pressure, external deficits and negative real rates keep policy room uneven.

Indonesia is the clearest example. Growth was better than expected, but J.P. Morgan still calls the 2H outlook cautious because fiscal and monetary conditions tighten while the current-account deficit widens. That combination keeps BI in tightening-bias territory despite a softer headline CPI print.

Thailand shows a different mix. CPI is lower than expected and BOT hold is the base case, but the rates strategy debate focuses on curve shape and long-end supply rather than front-end hikes. The Philippines is weaker in growth but still constrained by inflation and external pressure. This divergence is why the dashboard should use country-specific policy and FX panels instead of one ASEAN policy label.

Views

J.P. Morgan J.P. Morgan raised Indonesia’s 2026 growth forecast to 5.0% but still expects 2H26 growth to slow to 4.6% from 5.4% in 1H26; it also raises Indonesia’s current-account deficit forecast and expects BI to pause in August before hiking 25bp in September to 6.00%.

J.P. Morgan For Thailand, J.P. Morgan keeps BOT policy at 1.00% through 2027 but flags 50bp upside risk in 2027 if growth surprises. For the Philippines, it cuts 2026 growth to 3.5% from 4.0% after a weaker GDP outturn.

Maybank FX Maybank’s ASEAN FX deck differentiates FX fundamentals: MYR has stronger fundamental backing, PHP has inflation/external pressure, THB has structural shifts weighing in, IDR is framed as stabilization and SGD is the safe-haven within ASEAN.

03
EM local rates: tactical support, but valuations argue for carry and relative value
Supportive carry does not equal broad yield-compression call

The EM rates backdrop is the cleanest place to connect macro to trade ideas. Standard Chartered gives the practical framework: lower oil, stable USD and softer inflation help EM LCY markets, but valuations are already rich. That means the best trades should come from carry, roll and curve/relative value rather than an indiscriminate long-duration position.

BofA/KKP’s long-history real-rate note is useful as the structural anchor. EM Asia real rates have compressed since the Asian Financial Crisis as policy credibility and integration improved, but they have stayed more positive and idiosyncratic than developed-market real rates. This supports the idea that EM Asia still offers alpha and diversification, but country selection matters.

UBS reinforces the same conclusion from the FX side: if EM FX carry is crowded and dependent on Fed help, rates carry in selected countries can offer a superior risk/reward. The dashboard should therefore let rates trades stand on their own merits—carry, curve shape, local liquidity, supply and policy pricing—rather than relying only on a broad EM beta story.

Views

Standard Chartered Standard Chartered sees August as a constructive tactical window for EM LCY rates because lower oil, stable USD, softer inflation and light positioning reduce immediate hike pressure. But rich valuations limit broad yield compression; therefore carry, rolldown and idiosyncratic differentiation should drive returns.

BofA / KKP BofA/KKP’s Asia real-rates framework says EM Asia long-term real rates have declined over 76 years but stayed more persistently positive than in advanced economies, while correlations with US real rates have risen but often remain below 50%.

UBS UBS explicitly prefers rates carry over FX carry in parts of EM after the selloff, naming Mexico, India and South Africa as more compelling examples than broad EM FX carry.

04
Flows and technicals: improving, but tactical rather than structural
Bond flows are better; equity flows are ETF-heavy and headline-sensitive

Flows should be shown as a bridge between macro and trades. The source set does not support a structural reallocation story yet. It supports a tactical story: investors are returning to EM debt after the selloff because yields are attractive and core-yield pressure has eased, but flows remain headline-sensitive and positioning is not uniformly rebuilt.

Standard Chartered and HSBC are especially useful together. Standard Chartered shows the EM debt fund streak and the rebound in LCY debt funds, while HSBC stresses that KTB demand depends on hedging economics. The implication is that positive inflows alone are not enough; the cost of hedging, cross-currency basis, local supply and country-specific technicals can override the broad flow impulse.

J.P. Morgan adds breadth across EM asset classes: both bonds and equities saw inflows, but equity inflows were mostly ETF-led. For an EM rates/FX dashboard, this helps explain why improved risk appetite is supportive but not a reason to add equity-style broad beta into Trade Ideas.

Views

Standard Chartered Standard Chartered says EM debt funds extended the inflow streak to nine consecutive weeks, but foreign investor purchases of LCY debt moderated and THB debt saw selling. It expects flows to improve as US rates headwinds ease, but notes that any rebound could remain skewed toward DM assets.

HSBC HSBC calls broad-based EM inflows tactical, not structural. It says high-frequency data show net inflows across regions from lower core yields and higher EM carry, but flows remain sensitive to Fed-hike risk and hedging costs, especially for KTB demand.

J.P. Morgan J.P. Morgan’s EM Flows Weekly shows EM bond and equity flows both improved at the start of August, but the equity inflow was heavily ETF-driven and non-resident flow patterns remain uneven by country.

05
EM FX and ASEAN FX: the tradeable message is differentiation
Carry, reserves, external balances and hedging costs separate winners from losers

The EM FX section should be written as a differentiation story rather than a simple carry-positive story. UBS accepts that carry has worked, but warns that the micro underpinnings are deteriorating. EM central banks are more willing to lean against currency appreciation, growth differentials are challenged by a resilient US, and China’s export penetration can pressure non-China EM manufacturing and FX.

ASEAN is particularly mixed. Maybank’s title—“The Less Favored EM”—is important because it stops the dashboard from over-selling ASEAN FX. MYR has the best fundamental backing in that deck, SGD is the safe haven, but PHP and THB face external, inflation or structural headwinds, and IDR needs stabilization as a theme rather than a clean appreciation story.

The practical implication for Trade Ideas is that explicit FX trades should be treated carefully. Where no entry, target or stop exists, the view should remain a bias or macro framework. For ASEAN FX, the stronger content belongs in EM Macro Backdrop and Source Audit unless the source gives a tradeable recommendation.

Views

UBS UBS keeps a selective EM FX stance. It prefers COP over CLP, HUF over PLN, tactically BRL over MXN, long TRY optionality, and an outright short IDR. It argues EM carry gains can slow without Fed help.

Maybank FX Maybank frames ASEAN FX as less favored relative to other EM carry opportunities. It differentiates MYR as fundamentally backed, PHP as pressured by inflation and external balances, THB as weighed down by structural shifts, IDR as a stabilization story and SGD as a safe haven.

BofA / KKP BofA/KKP emphasizes that FX deposits and domestic capital-flight channels have been consistent headwinds. FX deposits can act as augmented reserves within central-bank reach, but they also reveal private-sector dollar preference when rate differentials favor USD holdings.
