Selective Carry, Not Broad Beta | Audience Dashboard

Aug 2026
Market setup: global growth is resilient, long-end rates remain the key constraint, and EM opportunities are concentrated in selective carry, curve and policy-pricing trades.
Global Macro Thesis

The global backdrop is still constructive enough for carry and selected duration trades, but not clean enough for broad EM beta. The key constraint remains whether long-end US yields are contained by softer data and Treasury buybacks, or whether fiscal and term-premium risk forces another bear steepening episode.

01
Global growth: resilient enough, but not a clean Goldilocks regime
Growth still holds, but the support is narrower than the headline suggests

The starting point remains that the global economy has not broken. Morgan Stanley still shows global GDP growth of 3.2% in 2026 and 3.3% in 2027, with EM growth higher at 4.5% and 4.4%. That keeps the dashboard in an opportunity-seeking frame rather than a defensive recession frame.

The tension is that the growth support is not broad or unconditional. US demand, capex and AI-linked activity are still doing much of the work, while UBS warns that EM leadership remains narrow and concentrated around North Asia tech. This is why the setup can support carry and selected duration trades without becoming a broad EM beta call.

Views

Morgan Stanley Constructive global forecasts and a pro-risk allocation bias, with US equities still preferred and MSCI EM target at 1,800.

HSBC Not a pure risk-off setup, but a market caught between Goldilocks and long-end pain.

UBS EM breadth is less convincing; EM FX needs Fed help and stronger domestic breadth to perform more broadly.

02
Policy makers are trying to contain the long end, not remove fiscal risk
UST buybacks can ease pressure, but they do not solve the deficit problem

The new global-rates papers sharpen the long-end message. BofA/KKP frames UST yields around 5% as a level that policy makers do not want to see pushed much higher, while HSBC shows the Treasury responding with larger long-end buybacks. The market read-through is not that fiscal risk is solved; it is that policy is trying to reduce the immediate pressure point.

That distinction matters for portfolios. If the long end rallies because Treasury buybacks and softer data support duration, EM local debt can breathe. If the long end sells off again because term premium, fiscal concern or inflation risk returns, EM duration and EM FX will be hit quickly.

Views

BofA / KKP UST long-end yield around 5% is treated as a key policy pain threshold because higher yields hit fiscal credibility and risk appetite.

HSBC Treasury doubled long-end buybacks, adding at least USD14bn of support, but debt-management policy has limits and does not resolve underlying fiscal pressures.

Morgan Stanley US 30Y yields crossing 5.2% keeps long-end rates at the center of cross-asset risk.

03
Fed and USD: peak hawkish fear is easing, but the market is still data-dependent
A friendlier front end helps carry, but it is not a full easing-cycle call

The Fed channel is less hostile than before, but it is not cleanly dovish. HSBC argues that the Sep-Jan FOMC curve has asymmetric steepening potential because the market may still have to price multi-hike risk if inflation stays sticky. J.P. Morgan has neutralized long USD after payrolls, but still makes clear that the dollar remains tied to near-term Fed pricing.

The portfolio implication is to keep EM exposure selective. Carry can still work when the front end is supported and the dollar is stable, but a single strong CPI or payroll print can quickly reprice Fed risk, widen hedging costs and reduce risk appetite.

Views

HSBC Opens a Sep-Jan FOMC OIS steepener; even a September hold may not remove later hike risk.

J.P. Morgan Neutralized long USD after payrolls but keeps carry central and remains data-dependent.

UBS EM FX performance needs a dovish or unchanged Fed to broaden beyond narrow carry leaders.

04
Risk appetite: enough for selective carry, not enough for broad EM beta
Flows are better, but country selection still matters

Risk appetite is no longer hostile to EM, but the flow evidence is still tactical. HSBC says investor interest in carry has firmed and higher G4 rates do not automatically trigger EM local-debt outflows unless accompanied by risk aversion or a stronger dollar. Standard Chartered similarly sees a constructive tactical window for EM LCY rates.

The unevenness is important. Korea and Indonesia saw inflows in the latest HSBC data, while Thailand and India saw outflows. This means the flow section should not be read as a broad allocation wave into EM; it is better framed as carry-seeking demand that still depends on hedging cost, FX stability and local technicals.

Views

HSBC Carry demand has improved; higher G4 rates are not fatal if dollar/risk aversion do not rise.

Standard Chartered Lower energy prices, USD stability and softer inflation support tactical EM LCY exposure.

J.P. Morgan EM bond and equity flows improved, but equity inflows were ETF-heavy.

EM Macro Thesis

EM local markets have a better tactical window from lower energy prices, stable USD and softer inflation, but valuation and flows argue for country selection. ASEAN should be read as a differentiated macro/FX story, not a single regional trade.

01
ASEAN macro: the fog is lifting, but the region is still energy- and FX-sensitive
Improving sentiment does not erase country-level vulnerabilities

ASEAN still deserves a constructive but selective treatment. Maybank’s message that the fog is lifting is useful because global sentiment improved and uncertainty eased, while lower crude reduced part of the pressure. But the same evidence shows why ASEAN cannot be treated as one uniform trade: Thailand, Singapore, the Philippines and Vietnam remain net energy importers, while Malaysia is the only net energy exporter in the Maybank table.

BofA/KKP adds the reserves and capital-flight angle. Indonesia and the Philippines are more vulnerable because current-account deficits and capital outflows make FX defense more costly. Thailand has a stronger reserves buffer, but that does not mean the currency is immune; it means the reaction function differs.

Views

Maybank ASEAN macro conditions are improving, but energy exposure remains a key differentiator.

BofA / KKP ASEAN FX defense is constrained by reserve ammunition and domestic capital-flight channels.

J.P. Morgan Indonesia beat growth forecasts, Thailand has low inflation/BOT hold, and the Philippines has the weakest growth surprise.

02
EM local rates: supportive carry, but no rush to add broad duration
Valuation and long-end risk keep the playbook selective

This section should look like the v3 Macro Backdrop section: narrative first, then views, then numbers. The message is that EM local rates have a tactical window from lower oil, stable USD and softer inflation, but valuations are not cheap enough for a broad duration rally.

HSBC APAC Rates adds an important regional nuance: Asia high-yielders such as PHP, IDR and INR outperformed because their yield buffers helped absorb the modest rise in long-end US rates. But HSBC is explicit that it is in no rush to add much more duration. Standard Chartered reaches a similar conclusion: carry and roll remain supportive, while valuation limits broad yield compression.

Views

HSBC Asia high-yielders outperformed on yield buffers and FX strength, but HSBC is in no rush to add more duration risk.

Standard Chartered EM LCY rates have a constructive tactical window, but carry, relative value and country differentiation should drive returns.

J.P. Morgan Fair-value work suggests EM local yields are close to fair value, reinforcing that valuation discipline matters.

03
Asia rates: China and India are preferred, but India duration is hedged
HSBC likes long-end IGBs but pays INR NDOIS against hike repricing risk

The APAC rates story is not simply “buy Asia duration.” HSBC’s preference is more nuanced: China and India bonds remain preferred, but the new India duration idea is paired with a paid INR NDOIS hedge because India hike expectations look too low versus peers. This is exactly the type of logic the Trade Ideas tab should preserve.

For Thailand, the source set also keeps two separate views alive. HSBC likes the THB 2s10s NDOIS steepener because the front end is anchored while the long end faces supply and seasonal pressure. Standard Chartered likes 5Y THOR/belly support from local liquidity and local demand. These are not the same trade, but they can coexist: front-end and belly are supported more than the long end.

HSBC Buy India 30Y IGB but pay India 5Y NDOIS because rate-hike expectations may rebuild.

HSBC THB 2s10s NDOIS steepener remains open; current around 87–88bp versus entry 84bp.

04
Flows: carry-seeking demand has improved, but the country map is uneven
The flow signal is supportive, not structural

The flows section should be a bridge to trade ideas. HSBC’s 21 August compass improves the tone: investor interest in carry has firmed, and higher G4 yields alone may not be enough to pull money out of EM local debt. But the same table shows a very uneven country map.

This is why the dashboard should not state that EM local debt is receiving a broad structural inflow. It should say that carry-seeking flows are improving in selected markets, especially where FX stability and hedging economics allow investors to hold positions.

Views

HSBC Carry-seeking flows improved; Türkiye debt is a notable beneficiary.

Standard Chartered Supportive carry and light positioning favor tactical EM LCY exposure.

J.P. Morgan EM bond flows were positive, but equity flows were ETF-heavy.

05
EM FX: carry works, but crowded positioning and ASEAN differentiation matter
FX views should remain country-specific unless the source gives an explicit trade

EM FX remains the most fragile part of the carry story. UBS shows that carry has worked, but the breadth of positive EM FX total returns is narrow. J.P. Morgan’s carry ranking gives a stronger numeric backbone, with TRY, BRL, COP, MXN, IDR and INR among high-carry currencies, but high carry does not automatically mean attractive risk-adjusted carry.

For ASEAN, Maybank’s “less favored EM” label should remain in the dashboard. MYR has the best fundamental backing in the deck, SGD is the safe haven, while THB, PHP and IDR need more careful treatment because of structural, inflation, external-balance or stabilization risks.

Views

UBS Selective EM FX stance; carry gains may slow without Fed help.

J.P. Morgan Carry ranking helps identify yield, but not all high-yield FX is a clean buy.

Maybank ASEAN FX is differentiated and generally less favored than higher-carry EM alternatives.
