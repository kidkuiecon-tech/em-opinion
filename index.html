FOMC 16 Sep 2026: unanimous 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00%
Global Macro Thesis

The September FOMC delivered a unanimous 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00% with higher dots, and the post-FOMC papers read it as a credibility win rather than the start of an open-ended cycle. The UST curve bear-flattened in the week to 18 Sep (2y +10bp, 5y +7bp, 10y +3bp, 30y −2bp), and HSBC argues the hike has freed the USD from the debasement narrative. For EM, this matters because year-to-date local-currency returns came almost entirely from FX, not duration: a firmer USD raises the hurdle for unhedged local exposure. Two caveats keep this from being a one-way call: JPM and HSBC both judge the 5s30s flattening as already stretched, and oil/El Niño remain a two-sided macro risk whose path the houses disagree on.

Evidence base: 52 September papers used (7 post-FOMC, 1 survey fielded pre-FOMC, 43 pre-FOMC baseline, 1 prior-month reference). Excluded: 1 duplicate, 1 out-of-period (2021) file. 5 files listed in the brief/prior draft were not received and no claims are drawn from them.

01
Post-FOMC regime: delivery restored Fed credibility
Post-FOMC layer: JPM (18 & 22 Sep), HSBC (22 Sep) · pre-FOMC baseline: Citi, MS, GS, HSBC

Before the meeting the debate was whether the Fed would validate an aggressive curve. Citi economists switched to a hike call after the August core CPI (+0.29% MoM), Morgan Stanley expected 25bp with no dissents, and Goldman expected a hike driven less by the economics than by the risk that holding would undermine Fed credibility. Market pricing on 16 Sep implied roughly four hikes over twelve months.

The outcome matched the hawkish end of expectations. JPMorgan reports a unanimous 25bp hike to 3.75–4.00%, higher dots, and Chair Warsh describing the move as removing 'a dose of accommodation'. JPM's key read-through is that Warsh's price-stability commitment is now backed by delivery, easing credibility concerns. JPM still expects the next hike in December and raised its YE26 UST targets.

Post-FOMC pricing moved in a telling direction: the policy rate rose, but 12-month US hike pricing fell from about 98bp (16 Sep) to 89bp (22 Sep). Delivery reduced the need for the market to price extra insurance. The same softening appears in Asian low-yielder front ends (Thailand, Malaysia, Taiwan), which is relevant for local-rates positioning.

Views

JPMorgan Unanimous 25bp hike; dots higher; credibility concerns eased. Next hike expected in December; YE26 2y target 4.70% (+40bp), 10y 5.05% (+20bp).

HSBC Pre-FOMC: the USD did not need the Fed to 'out-hawk' the market, only to keep the priced path plausible. Post-FOMC: hawkish dots and an activist press conference delivered that.

Morgan Stanley Pre-FOMC call (14 Sep) for a September hike with no dissents and one more in December was confirmed by the outcome.

Citi Pre-FOMC: economists expected the hike to be presented as a 'calibration', not the start of a sustained cycle.

02
UST curve: bear-flattening happened — and may already be overdone
Front-end repricing drove the move; two houses see valuation stretched

The curve verdict is factual, not interpretive: JPMorgan describes the week as one in which 'the curve bearishly flattened', with 2y yields +10bp, 5y +7bp and 10y +3bp while the long end fell 2bp. DM curves broadly twisted 10–20bp flatter. The move was front-end led, consistent with Citi's pre-FOMC observation that higher energy prices were pushing yields up 'all front-end led' and with Citi's historical finding that curves tend to flatten around a first hike.

The caveat matters for positioning. JPM's model shows 5s30s UST, Bund and gilt curves 5–15bp too flat after a sharp term-premium compression. HSBC went further before the meeting, calling the flattening 'gone too far' and opening a 5s30s steepener. JPM itself is not rushing to add duration and hedges its curve views for Fed expectations.

For EM, a front-end-led selloff is a different shock from a long-end fiscal one: it supports the USD and raises hedging costs, but it does not by itself signal a term-premium blowout. The long end remains the constraint — 10y UST traded around 5% into the meeting — but the latest evidence argues against extrapolating further flattening.

Views

JPMorgan 'The curve bearishly flattened this week'; 5s30s screens 5–15bp too flat on JPM's model. Neutral duration; 2Y-fwd 2s10s steepener hedged with 1Y-fwd flattener.

HSBC Flattening 'has now gone too far' with ~100bp of tightening priced; 5s30s UST steepener (entry 52bp, target 90bp, stop 30bp).

Citi Curves tend to flatten around first hikes; higher oil implies flatter curves. Citi Rates stays long the 20y with a YE26 10y target of 3.90% — a clear divergent duration view.

UBS YTD 10y rise: UST +79bp, Bund +66bp, gilts ≈+90bp; the UST move is mainly real yields.

03
USD: from debasement fear to tactical strength
Post-FOMC evidence supports a firmer USD; medium-term diversification arguments survive

The most important narrative shift is in the dollar. HSBC's post-FOMC view is that the hawkish meeting 'freed the USD from a debasement theme': the hike, the dots and the press conference did not force a dovish repricing, and with Fed credibility improving, FX markets will look for vulnerabilities elsewhere — EUR, GBP and JPY, particularly if energy prices stay high. SCBT adds that real yields are doing most of the lifting and that higher oil benefits the US as a net exporter.

Pre-FOMC views were split. JPMorgan's global FX team stayed bullish USD into the meeting against low-yielders, while its Asia team saw only 'bounded upside' for the USD and moved to market weight Asia FX. Maybank expected risks to tilt toward USD weakness and preferred selling rallies with DXY capped near 100 — the outcome it viewed as least likely (hike plus hawkish guidance) is what happened, and HSBC puts DXY at 100.33 on 22 Sep.

The medium-term argument has not disappeared. UBS expects only modest dollar gains after a hike and still sees a case for diversification into EM, and Citi's LatAm team frames the current regime as a weaker-dollar one. The practical formulation: stress-test unhedged EM FX against a firmer USD, and require country-specific carry or balance-of-payments support.

Views

HSBC Post-FOMC: USD freed from debasement; sell GBP-USD at 1.3364 (target 1.3010, stop 1.3540). EUR, GBP and JPY vulnerable if energy stays high.

Standard Chartered Real yields doing the heavy lifting; higher oil benefits the US as a net exporter; firmer USD behind part of the decline in EM LCY gains.

JPMorgan Global FX: stay bullish USD into FOMC vs low-yielders; carry remains the higher-conviction theme. Asia: 'bounded upside' for USD; MW Asia FX.

Maybank Pre-FOMC opposing view: risks tilted to USD weakness; sell USD on rallies with DXY 100 as a cap.

UBS 'No dollar holler': modest USD gains if the Fed hikes; more upside risk to EMFX if the Fed pauses than downside if it hikes.

04
Energy and El Niño: a two-sided inflation risk the houses disagree on
Oil path is the main forecast divergence; El Niño risk is broadly agreed

Energy is the swing factor for both the curve and EM country selection. Citi's central case is that the Strait of Hormuz reopens during 4Q26, taking Brent to about $65 in 2027 and setting up lower inflation and yields; a sustained disruption would instead push Brent to $110–120. HSBC has moved the other way, raising Brent forecasts on a 'disrupted new normal' in which Hormuz is persistently impaired. Citi's CEEMEA desk documents why the risk is live: Houthi control of Bab el-Mandeb and the 11 Sep strike on Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline.

On weather, the evidence is consistent. Citi's tracker shows a Super El Niño already under way (ONI ≈2.3°C), with below-normal rainfall across Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, the Philippines and India, and EM Asia food-inflation momentum led by Thailand. UBS estimates that oil at $110 would add 70–90bp to EM headline CPI into year-end, with El Niño effects landing into 2027.

The macro implication runs through policy: energy and food keep front-end pricing sticky, cap the room for local duration rallies in oil- and food-sensitive markets, and sharpen the split between North Asia (AI export surpluses) and South/Southeast Asia (energy importers).

Views

Citi Base case: Hormuz reopens in 4Q26, Brent $65 in 2027; tail $110–120. Super El Niño bullish for ags; 2027 CPO forecast raised to RM4.8k/mt.

HSBC Higher-for-longer Brent on a persistently impaired Hormuz; TTF forecasts raised with upside risk.

UBS Oil at $110 could add ~70–90bp to EM CPI into YE; prefers North Asia over South Asia and has added El Niño-sensitive hedges.

JPMorgan Asian CB hawkishness is driven by AI-led growth broadening, not inflation — which remains quiescent for now.

EM Macro Thesis

EM is resilient but conviction has narrowed. The asset class held up better than in past geopolitical shocks, yet its year-to-date local-currency return came from FX while duration lost money — so the post-FOMC dollar matters more than the Fed hike itself. The evidence does not support a blanket rotation into hard currency: positioning still favours hard currency, but investor preference is moving toward local debt and valuations have improved. The investable answer is selection — North Asia FX over energy-importing South/Southeast Asia, local curves where valuation and policy pricing give a cushion, and hedged or hard-currency exposure where FX beta is not being paid.

01
EM macro: resilience survives, conviction narrows
Latest surveys and conference reads: constructive but low-conviction, with more cash

HSBC's quarterly survey (102 institutions, USD424bn EM AUM, fielded 3 Aug–16 Sep) shows bullish respondents down to 50% from 60%, bears back at 5%, and net sentiment at 45% — still the 16th consecutive positive reading. Risk appetite rose, but so did cash, to 5.7% of AUM, the highest since December 2023. Note that the survey closed on FOMC day, so it captures pre-decision positioning.

JPMorgan's EM & Frontier conference (15–17 Sep, ~900 investors) reached a similar conclusion: comfort with fundamentals and yields, but conviction 'does not seem to be high'. The live poll named an AI/MAG-7 meltdown as the top risk (48%), and there was no consensus 2027 outperformer.

Strategy houses agree on the direction but not the pace. UBS expects EM multi-asset outperformance to extend into year-end as an 'evolution, not a revolution', resting on valuations, North Asian surpluses and strong credit impulses. DB finds EM local markets held up considerably better than in previous geopolitical shocks. DBS/Bain keep a 4.8% 2026–35 growth base case for Southeast Asia but flag Indonesia, the Philippines and Thailand as the most exposed in a downside scenario.

Views

HSBC Net sentiment 45%; bulls 50% / neutral 44% / bears 5%; cash 5.7% of AUM; LatAm the favoured region, Asia preferred for equities.

JPMorgan Conference: near-term resilience expected, low conviction; frontier local markets still most compelling but more crowded.

UBS EM outperformance can extend into YE; rotation into EM an evolution, not a revolution.

DBS / Bain SEA 2026–35 base case 4.8% (from 5.1%); ID, PH, TH most exposed in the downside.

02
Local vs hard currency: positioning favours hard, preference is rotating to local
FX carried local returns; a firmer USD raises the hurdle but valuations have improved

Three houses independently show that EM local returns have been an FX story. DB: EMFI FX-unhedged returned 1.5% in Q3 to date, from a 1.6% FX gain against a 1.1% duration loss. HSBC: the index-level year-to-date gain in EM local debt came almost entirely from currency, not bonds — which makes broad USD strength the key risk. SCBT: a firmer USD is behind part of the decline in EM LCY gains.

That argues for a higher hurdle on unhedged local exposure, but not for a wholesale switch. In HSBC's survey, positioning still leans to hard currency (external debt net overweight 22% versus local debt net underweight 20%), yet local debt is now the preferred fixed-income sub-asset for 53% of respondents, up from 18%, while hard-currency preference fell to 42% from 55%. DB judges that value has been restored in real rates and term premia. JPMorgan stays market weight across GBI-EM, CEMBI and EMBIGD.

Hard currency has its own support: Asia high-grade yields reached about 6.0% with JPM seeing the spread path of least resistance as tighter, and sovereign spreads remain tight. The reconciled view: use local where valuation, policy pricing or curve shape give a cushion; use hard currency or hedged local where FX beta is not being paid.

Views

Deutsche Bank Q3 return FX-driven; valuations restored via real rates and term premia; constructive but selective — belly over outright duration.

HSBC Local debt returns FX-driven; foreign inflows tapered through Aug–Sep; survey preference shifted to local (53%) while positioning is still UW local.

JPMorgan MW GBI-EM, CEMBI and EMBIGD; Asia HG yields ≈6.0% with spreads expected to grind tighter absent further oil or Fed shocks.

Standard Chartered EM LCY still sees inflows despite waning valuation appeal; benchmark investors continue to favour ex-Asian markets.

03
Asia FX: North Asia surpluses versus energy-importer vulnerability
Country dispersion dominates the regional call

BofA's scorecard captures the split. AI-led semiconductor exports support KRW, TWD and SGD, while INR, IDR and PHP face oil-shock headwinds. The balance-of-payments revisions are stark: Korea's 2026 current-account forecast rose 14.5pp of GDP since March, while Thailand's fell 3.4pp — the largest downgrade in the region. BofA's Korea work shows corporates sold a net USD187bn of FX in 1H26, more than three times the prior-year pace.

Trade expressions line up with that map. JPMorgan moved to market weight Asia FX by going overweight CNH against an underweight in THB, and stays long INR against PHP and IDR. UBS is long CNH and KRW against USD and EUR and long USD against IDR and PHP. HSBC sees the pace of CNY fixings implying ~5.8% annualised appreciation, which makes long RMB attractive again despite negative carry.

The Philippines and Indonesia are the pressure points. JPM notes BSP has sold USD21.8bn of reserves since November to cap USD/PHP below 62, and Indonesia's net reserves fell to USD77bn (−USD25bn YTD). A new, technocratic Indonesian finance minister improves fiscal credibility, according to Maybank and JPM credit, but does not change the external arithmetic.

Views

BofA KRW/TWD/SGD supported by AI exports; INR/IDR/PHP exposed to oil; Thai current-account downgrade a THB headwind. Preference: long INR & MYR vs short THB.

JPMorgan MW Asia FX via OW CNH vs UW THB; long INR vs PHP, IDR; long TWD vs SGD; neutral KRW after the recent overshoot.

UBS Long CNH & KRW vs USD & EUR; long USD vs IDR & PHP; North Asia preferred over South Asia.

HSBC RMB fixings imply ~5.8% annualised appreciation; USD-VND to ~26,400 by YE but risks skew to a stronger VND on FTSE EM inclusion.

04
Asia local rates: hawkish on growth, selective on duration
SCBT post-FOMC country map, cross-checked against JPM, HSBC and Maybank

Asian central banks turned more hawkish in Q3 because the AI export impulse is broadening into the domestic economy, not because inflation has taken off (JPM). BoK has delivered back-to-back hikes and SCBT sees money markets pricing another ~125bp to a 4.25% terminal; RBI minutes opened the door to an October hike; BNM changed guidance toward a 4Q26/1Q27 hike. HSBC remains patient on outright duration and prefers paid positions in Malaysia, India and Japan.

SCBT's post-FOMC map is the most granular: neutral on Indian and Indonesian bonds; KRW rates outperforming the US with the KTB curve shifting to flattening into Q4; Singapore facing higher pass-through from US rates; Philippine bonds set to underperform on PHP weakness; value surfacing further down the MGS curve. Thailand is covered separately in section 06.

Malaysia is where the evidence converges on a possible turning point. MGS sold off 50–65bp across 5–20y in 3Q26. Citi stopped out of a 5y MYR receiver (−25bp) while JPM took profit on the mirror-image payer (+37bp), arguing that hawkish BNM pivots have historically marked local yield peaks. Maybank upgraded MGS to mildly bullish. HSBC still pays 2y MYR on year-end funding tightness — so the call is about timing, not direction.

Views

Standard Chartered Country-by-country: IN neutral, ID neutral, KR outperforms US, SG higher US pass-through, PH underperform, MY value down the curve.

05
What's Priced In: FOMC delivery trimmed Asian hike premia
JPM WPI 16 Sep (pre) vs 22 Sep (post) — market pricing vs JPM 3Q27 forecasts

JPMorgan's What's Priced In compares FRA-implied policy paths with its own forecasts; a positive gap means the market prices more hikes (or fewer cuts) than JPM expects. Comparing the 16 Sep and 22 Sep editions isolates the FOMC effect: 12-month hike pricing fell by roughly 9–12bp in Thailand, Malaysia and Taiwan, while India and Korea barely moved.

The screen is most useful where the gap is large and the local story does not justify it. Thailand stands out: the market prices about 33bp of BoT hikes over twelve months while JPM expects no change through 3Q27 — a 34bp gap. Outside Asia, Poland (+92bp), Czech Republic (+87bp) and Brazil (+83bp) show the largest gaps versus JPM forecasts.

The gap is not a mechanical buy signal; it has to be read against valuation (DB, SCBT), FX sensitivity and oil exposure. It does, however, identify where local front ends carry a cushion if the hawkish global impulse fades.

06
Thailand: the clearest house divergence in the pack
Cheap duration on local technicals vs underweight on external and policy grounds

Thailand is where the September papers disagree most, which makes it the most useful section for a Thai investor. The constructive case comes from SCBT: BoT is on a prolonged hold, banks are recycling excess liquidity into bonds, THOR has outperformed on BoT/Fed divergence, net supply is lower, and 10Y THOR screens about 3 standard deviations (≈30bp) above fair value. HSBC keeps Thailand among its preferred duration markets once pre-conditions are met, while running a 2s10s steepener on supply and seasonality.

The cautious case is also well-argued. JPMorgan is underweight ThaiGBs and THB in its GBI-EM model: energy and capital-goods imports weigh on the current account, BoT is dovish, and the ThaiGB curve is the steepest in the region without an obvious FX problem. BofA cut its 2026 current-account forecast by 3.4pp to a small deficit and prefers short THB. ThaiGBs lost 10.1% year to date in USD terms, the weakest in HSBC's Asia table, and foreign investors were net sellers in the latest week.

The market sits between the two: it prices about 33bp of BoT hikes that JPM does not expect. Macro remains soft — UBS hears growth expectations of 2.6–2.7% with weak transmission of the investment cycle to households and SMEs — while equity sentiment is notably constructive on AI/data-centre and PDP 2026 themes. Food inflation is the swing risk: Citi finds Thailand leading EM Asia food-inflation momentum under El Niño.

Views

Standard Chartered BoT prolonged hold; THOR outperforms; 10Y THOR ≈3 SD / ≈30bp above fair value; low net supply anchors bonds.

JPMorgan UW ThaiGBs and UW THB in GBI-EM; lean against long-end cheapness on ASW pending oil stabilisation and budget clarity; short THB 'long in the tooth' into year-end seasonals.

HSBC Receive 2y / pay 10y THB NDOIS (target 110bp, stop 85bp); Thailand on the preferred duration list once conditions allow.

BofA Largest C/A downgrade in Asia (−3.4pp); prefers long INR & MYR vs short THB.

UBS Macro 'balanced' (GDP 2.6–2.7%); equity sentiment notably constructive on AI/data centres and PDP 2026.

Scenario and Opposing Views

This tab reconciles the source set rather than forcing one house view. Most conflicts are temporal (pre- vs post-FOMC) or about horizon (local technicals vs external balance); the oil path is the one genuine forecast disagreement.

01
Consensus: EM resilient, conviction narrow
Broad agreement on direction; disagreement on pace and instrument

Across the source set, no house argues EM is broken: growth has surprised positively (BofA), local markets held up better than in past shocks (DB), and investors hold both risk appetite and cash (HSBC). Equally, no house supports indiscriminate beta. The shared constraints are the dollar after the FOMC, the oil path and front-end policy pricing.

Views

Consensus Selective EM risk remains valid; carry works where valuation and external buffers are adequate.

Consensus North Asia (AI surpluses) preferred over energy-importing South/Southeast Asia.

Consensus El Niño and energy keep inflation risk two-sided into 2027.
